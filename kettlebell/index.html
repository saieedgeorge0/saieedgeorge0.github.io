⚠️ Your Compensation Pattern — Read This First
Your lower back, hamstrings, and adductors tend to take over for your glutes and quads. Every lower body cue in this program addresses three corrections:

1. External rotation torque — screwing feet into the floor, pushing knees out. This fires glute max and glute med.
2. Vertical shin / hip-dominant hinging — sitting down (not back) in squats loads quads; pushing hips back (not bending knees) in hinges loads glutes over lower back.
3. Brace before you move — a locked core prevents spinal erectors from hijacking every rep.

Warm-Up
🟠
Glute Bridges
2 × 15
Glutes
▼
Setup & Cues
  • Lie on your back, feet flat hip-width apart, knees bent ~90°. Feet should be close enough that fingertips can just graze your heels.
  • Tilt your pelvis posteriorly (flatten your low back into the floor) before you lift — this prevents lower back extension from taking over.
  • Drive through the entire foot — heel, big toe mound, and pinky toe mound equally. Don't let the arch collapse inward (adductor compensation).
  • Squeeze glutes hard and drive hips up until body forms a straight line from shoulders to knees. Push knees slightly apart if they cave in.
  • At the top, hold for 1 full second and focus on squeezing the glutes — not just being in the up position.
  • If your hamstrings cramp instead of your glutes working, your feet are too far from your body — move them closer.
  • Lower slowly (2–3 seconds). Do not crash down.

Clamshells
2 × 15 per side · band optional
Glute Med
▼
Cues
  • Lie on your side, hips stacked, knees bent ~45°, feet together. Place a light band just above the knees.
  • Keep your pelvis completely still — do not rotate your hips open to create the movement. If pelvis rocks, you're using hip flexors and lower back, not glute medius.
  • Lift the top knee toward the ceiling as high as you can without the pelvis moving. Think "rotate from the hip joint only."
  • At the top, pause and squeeze. The working muscle should be felt in the side/back of the hip — not the inner thigh or low back.
  • Lower slowly. Do not let the knee crash back down.

KB Suitcase March
2 × 10 steps per side
Glute MedCore
▼
Cues
  • Hold one KB at your side (like a suitcase). Start light — this is a stability drill, not a loaded carry.
  • Stand tall: ribs down, not flared. Brace your core as if someone is about to push you sideways.
  • The goal is to keep your hips level as you march. Do not let the hip on the KB side drop — that drop means glute medius is not firing.
  • Slowly lift one knee to 90°, pause for 1 second at the top, then lower. Alternate sides.
🟠
Glute Step-Ups
2 × 10 per side
Glutes
▼
Cues
  • Use a box/step that puts your knee at ~90° when foot is on top. Place entire foot on the box — not just the toes.
  • Drive through the heel of the elevated foot before you push. Think: press the box into the floor with your heel.
  • Do NOT push off the back foot — it should barely touch the floor. All work comes from the front leg.
  • Drive the knee of the elevated leg out (in the direction of your toes) as you stand — keeps the glute engaged, prevents knee caving.
  • At the top, fully extend the hip and squeeze the glute. Do not lean torso forward excessively — that shifts load to the lower back.
  • Lower by hinging at the hip first, then the knee. Do not drop.
🟠
Quad Step-Ups
2 × 10 per side
Quads
▼
Cues
  • Same box height. Torso leans slightly more forward than glute step-ups — the emphasis shifts to knee extension (quad).
  • Focus on letting your knee travel forward over your toes as you rise — this loads the quad.
  • Drive through the mid-foot and toes (vs. the heel in glute step-ups). Think: "straighten my knee to stand up."
  • Even here, push the knee out in line with your toes — do not let it cave inward.
  • The burn should be felt at the front of the thigh, above the kneecap.
Main Workout

KB Deadlift
3 × 8–10
GlutesHamstringsLow back watch
▼
Setup
  • Stand with KB between your feet, centered at your mid-foot, feet hip-width apart. Toes can point slightly out (10–15°).
  • Before reaching down, set your spine: chest up, long neck. Brace your core 360° — breathe into your belly and sides, then brace like you're about to take a punch.
The Hinge
  • Push your hips back toward the wall behind you. Shins stay nearly vertical. This is a hip hinge, not a squat — knees bend only as much as needed to reach the handle.
  • Back must stay flat. Think "proud chest" and "long spine." If lower back rounds, you went too deep — stop higher.
The Lift
  • Grip the KB and take the slack out: squeeze hard, engage your lats ("protect your armpits"). This creates full-body tension before the KB leaves the floor.
  • Big breath in, brace hard, then push the floor away with your feet. Think "leg press" — drive the floor down, not the KB up.
  • Hips and shoulders should rise at the same rate — if hips shoot up first, lower back is taking over.
  • At the top: stand fully tall, hips fully extended, glutes squeezed. Do NOT lean back or hyperextend.
⚠️ Compensation Check If you feel this primarily in your hamstrings and not your glutes at lockout, focus harder on the hip drive and glute squeeze as you stand. If lower back is aching, your hips are shooting up first — slow down and keep hips and shoulders rising together.

KB Overhead Press
3 × 8 per side
ShouldersTriceps
▼
Cues
  • Clean the KB to rack position: KB resting on the back of your forearm, elbow tucked close to body at chest height, wrist straight and neutral.
  • Stand feet hip-width apart, ribs down (not flared). Brace your core and glutes throughout.
  • Press directly overhead in a slight arc — KB travels slightly behind your ear. Final position: KB directly over shoulder joint, arm fully locked out, bicep next to ear.
  • Do not let your lower back hyperextend to get the KB overhead. If you feel a big arch, shoulder mobility is the limiting factor.
  • Keep wrist stacked over forearm. Do not let the KB tip backward and bend the wrist.
  • Press to full lockout. Pull KB back down to rack with control — the eccentric builds shoulder stability.

KB Floor Press
3 × 10 · Added: chest coverage
ChestTricepsNew exercise
▼
Cues
  • Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat. Hold one KB in each hand at chest height, elbows at ~45° from torso (not flared out to 90°).
  • Press your upper back firmly into the floor. Actively squeeze shoulder blades together and down before pressing.
  • Press the KBs up to full arm extension. Think "spread the floor with your shoulder blades" — keep them retracted and depressed, not shrugging toward your ears.
  • At the top, fully lock out the elbows and squeeze the chest.
  • Lower with control until elbows rest gently on the floor. Do not bounce. Pause 1 second at the bottom, then press again.

KB Goblet Squat — Hold + Band
3 × 8 · 3-sec hold at bottom · band around knees
QuadsGlutesPrimary compensation focus
▼
Setup
  • Place a light-to-medium band just above your knees. Stand feet shoulder-width or slightly wider, toes pointed out 20–30°.
  • Hold the KB by the horns (the sides of the handle) at chest height, close to your sternum.
The Descent
  • Before squatting: big breath in, brace core 360°, and actively push knees OUT against the band. This external rotation torque must be maintained for the entire rep.
  • Sit down between your heels, not back. Your shins should travel forward (knees tracking over toes), which loads your quads. Sitting back shifts load to lower back and hamstrings.
  • Keep your chest tall and upright. Drive your elbows down toward your thighs at the bottom to stay upright.
  • Descend until hip crease is at or below knee level.
The 3-Second Hold
  • Continue pushing knees OUT against the band.
  • Feel the stretch in your inner thighs (adductors) — the goal is the stretch stays there, not in the lower back.
  • Try to "spread the floor" with your feet (split laterally) — you won't move, but this cue fires the glutes and creates hip stability.
  • Keep your core braced. Do not let your lower back round at the bottom.
The Ascent
  • Drive the floor away through your entire foot (heel + big toe mound + pinky toe mound). Think "leg press the floor down."
  • Keep pushing knees out as you rise — especially through the sticking point just above parallel.
  • Squeeze glutes hard at the top. Stand fully tall, hips fully extended.
⚠️ Compensation Check Feeling it mostly in your inner thighs? Knees are caving — push them out more aggressively. Feeling it mostly in hamstrings or lower back? You're sitting back instead of down — shift weight slightly toward your toes and let knees travel forward more.

KB Bent-Over Row
3 × 10 per side
LatsRhomboidsRear Delts
▼
Cues
  • Stand feet hip-width apart. Hinge at hips to ~45° torso angle. Rest non-working hand on your thigh or a bench for support. Spine neutral — no rounding.
  • Brace your core. Think "long spine from tailbone to crown."
  • Row by driving your elbow back toward your hip — not out to the side. Think "elbow to back pocket."
  • At the top, your elbow should be at or just above your torso. Squeeze lat and rhomboid hard. Think "pinch a pencil between your shoulder blades."
  • Do not rotate your torso to get the KB higher — that is lower back compensation. Keep hips square to the floor.
  • Lower with full control. Feel the lat stretch at the bottom. Pause briefly, then repeat.

KB Goblet Reverse Lunge
3 × 8 per side · Added: unilateral quad/glute
QuadsGlutesNew exercise
▼
Cues
  • Hold KB goblet-style (by the horns) at chest. Stand tall, feet hip-width apart.
  • Step back with one foot — not forward. The reverse lunge better loads the front leg and is more knee-friendly.
  • Back knee descends toward (but does not slam into) the floor. Land lightly on the ball of the back foot.
  • Front shin should be as vertical as possible — keep the front knee from traveling too far forward. This ensures front quad and glute are truly loaded.
  • Front thigh roughly parallel to the floor (or lower if comfortable). Torso upright.
  • Front knee tracks directly over your second or third toe. Push it outward if it caves.
  • Push through the heel of the front foot to stand. Squeeze the front glute as you rise. Fully extend the hip at the top.
⚠️ Compensation Check Feeling it in your lower back? Your torso is leaning too far forward — stay upright. Feeling it only in hamstrings, not quads? Your front shin is too far back — step back less so the shin is more vertical.

KB RDL (Romanian Deadlift)
3 × 10
GlutesHamstringsLow back watch
▼
Setup & Key Distinction
  • Hold one or two KBs in front of your thighs, feet hip-width apart. Soft, consistent bend in the knees throughout — this is not a squat. The knee bend does NOT increase as you lower.
The Hinge
  • Push your hips back toward the wall behind you. Imagine a rope attached to your hips pulling them backward. The entire movement is from the hip — not the knee.
  • As your hips go back, your torso lowers and KBs travel down the front of your thighs. Keep KBs dragging close to your legs throughout — "shave your shins."
  • Maintain a neutral spine throughout. Think "proud chest" — long and flat, no rounding in thoracic or lumbar spine.
  • Stop when you feel a deep, strong stretch in your glutes (not just hamstrings) — typically mid-shin to floor. Do not go further than where you can maintain a flat back.
The Return
  • Squeeze your glutes and drive your hips forward (not up). Think "hips to the wall in front of you."
  • The hip drive brings you back to standing — your torso follows the hips, not the other way around.
  • At the top, stand fully tall and squeeze glutes. Do not hyperextend the lower back.
⚠️ Compensation Check Feeling this primarily in your lower back on the way up? You're pulling with spinal erectors instead of driving with glutes — focus on the hip drive. Feeling sharp hamstring pull but no glute sensation? Try initiating more consciously from the glutes. Lower back rounding at the bottom? You went too deep — stop higher.

KB Single-Leg RDL
3 × 8 per side · Added: unilateral posterior chain
GlutesGlute MedNew exercise
▼
Cues
  • Hold one KB in the hand opposite to the standing leg (contralateral loading) — this fires the glute medius of the standing leg harder.
  • Soft bend in the standing knee throughout. Keep both hips square to the floor.
  • Same hip hinge as the bilateral RDL: push hips back, spine neutral. Your body acts as a see-saw pivoting at the hip of the standing leg.
  • Keep your hips square — do not let the hip of the raised leg rotate open toward the ceiling. If it opens, your IT band/TFL is compensating.
  • Lower until you feel a deep stretch in the glute of the standing leg, or until you can no longer keep hips square — whichever comes first.
  • Drive through the heel of the standing foot and squeeze the glute to return. Do not swing the back leg down — control it.
  • At the top, bring non-standing knee up to 90° and balance for 1 second to maximize glute engagement.

KB Hard-Style Swing
3 × 15
GlutesHamstringsLow back watch
▼
The Hinge vs. Squat Distinction
  • The swing is a hip hinge, not a squat. Knees bend slightly but do not drive forward. If your knees track significantly over your toes on the backswing, you're squatting the swing — this kills glute activation and loads the lower back.
  • Think: "push hips back, not knees down."
The Hike & Hinge
  • Hike the KB back between your legs like a center snapping a football. Forearms make contact with inner thighs on the backswing. At this point: hips are hinged back, spine is neutral, knees are soft.
The Hip Snap
  • From the hinge, explosively drive your hips forward — like slamming your hips into a wall in front of you. Your arms are just ropes; the hip snap launches the KB.
  • At the top (KB at chest/eye height): stand tall. Hips fully extended, glutes squeezed hard ("crack a walnut"), core braced, knees soft.
  • Do NOT lean back at the top. "Tall" means neutral spine — arching the lower back means you're hyperextending, not extending at the hip.
The Descent
  • Actively pull the KB back down as you push your hips back into the hinge — do not wait for gravity.
  • Re-hike it between the legs and immediately snap again.
⚠️ Compensation Check Feeling this in your lower back? Your swing is a squat-swing — knees are driving forward instead of hips going back. Video yourself from the side: shins should stay close to vertical on the backswing. The load should be felt in glutes at the top and glutes + hamstrings on the hinge.

Dead Bug
3 × 8 per side · Added: core anti-extension
CoreNew exercise
▼
Cues
  • Lie on your back. Press your lower back firmly into the floor and keep it there the entire set. The second your lower back arches off the floor, you have lost the exercise.
  • Raise arms straight toward the ceiling. Bring knees up to 90°, shins parallel to the floor (tabletop position).
  • Simultaneously lower one arm overhead toward the floor and extend the opposite leg, lowering heel toward the floor.
  • Move slowly — 3–4 seconds down, 3–4 seconds back up.
  • Lower only as far as you can with your lower back pressing into the floor. For many people this is only partway down at first — that's fine.
  • Return to start, switch sides.
⚠️ Compensation Check If your lower back peels off the floor, you're moving beyond your current core stability. Reduce range of motion — lower limbs only halfway until you build the strength to go further.

KB Calf Raise
3 × 15 · Added: calves
CalvesNew exercise
▼
Cues
  • Hold one KB at your side for loading.
  • Stand on the edge of a step or slightly elevated surface so your heels can drop below the level of your toes.
  • Lower your heels slowly (3 seconds) until you feel a full calf stretch.
  • Drive up on to the balls of your feet as high as possible. Pause at the top and squeeze.
  • Keep both feet equally weighted — do not shift to one side.

Sunday — Rest & Active Recovery
Light walking, stretching, or foam rolling. Priority mobility areas given your compensation patterns:
Recovery Priorities
  • Hip Flexor Stretch (kneeling lunge) — Tight hip flexors contribute directly to lower back overuse. In a half-kneeling position, push your hips forward gently. Hold 45–60 seconds per side.
  • Glute Figure-Four / Pigeon Stretch — Restores hip external rotation. Cross one ankle over the opposite knee and gently press down. This directly counteracts the adductor-dominance pattern. Hold 45–60 seconds per side.
  • Adductor Stretch (side lunge / frog) — Helps desensitize the adductors so they are less likely to compensate in squats. Wide stance, sink into one hip at a time.
  • Thoracic Spine Foam Rolling — Roll along the mid-back (between shoulder blades and lower ribs). Improving upper back extension reduces how much your lower back compensates during presses and squats. Spend 2–3 minutes here.
  • Quad Stretch (standing or lying) — After three days of loaded squat patterns, a gentle quad stretch maintains tissue length. Hold 30–45 seconds per side.
